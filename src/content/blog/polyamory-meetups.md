**In short:** A polyamory meetup is an open, regular gathering for people who are polyamorous or curious about it. You can find them through event platforms, regional groups on social networks, LGBTQ+ centres and online communities. They are not dating events but places for conversation, shared experience and the feeling of not being alone. The first time, listening is enough, and common group rules usually protect your privacy well.

Many people spend their first polyamorous years fairly alone. Nobody in their friend group lives this way, their family does not know, and their only conversation partners are forums and books. That works for a while. But at some point something is missing: people you can see in person who understand what it is about without you having to explain everything.

That is exactly what poly meetups, discussion circles and community events are for. This article explains how to find them and what to expect.

## Why local community helps

Online groups like the [polyamory community on Reddit](/en/blog/polyamory-reddit-community/) are valuable, but they have limits. In-person meetups bring something else:

- **Normality.** Seeing that very different people live polyamorously, with jobs, kids, hobbies and everyday lives, takes away a lot of uncertainty.
- **Lived experience.** How others deal with jealousy, scheduling or coming out is often learned faster in conversation than from articles.
- **Support.** In a crisis, for example after a [breakup](/en/blog/breakups-polyamory/), it helps to know people who do not need the situation explained first.
- **Friendships.** Many attendees say that what they mostly found there was friends, not partners.

## Which formats exist

| Format | What to expect |
|---|---|
| **Social meetup** | Casual gathering in a café or bar, usually monthly, with no set programme. Good for getting started. |
| **Discussion group** | Moderated circle on a topic such as jealousy or parenting. More structured and often more confidential. |
| **Workshop** | Guided event with exercises, often on communication or agreements. Sometimes paid. |
| **Picnic, games night, hike** | Social events without a topic, often open to partners and sometimes families. |
| **Online meetup** | Video calls for people in rural areas or with little time. |
| **Conferences and festivals** | Multi-day events with talks and workshops, more something for later. |

## Where to find meetups

**Event platforms.** On Meetup and similar platforms, many larger cities have groups for polyamory or ethical non-monogamy. Search for "polyamory", "poly", "open relationships" or "non-monogamy".

**Social networks.** Regional groups on social networks are often the most active place, especially for smaller cities. Many are private and need a short request to join.

**Reddit and forums.** Subreddits for your city or region and polyamory forums often contain hints about local meetups.

**LGBTQ+ centres and counselling services.** Many LGBTQ+ centres offer groups on relationship diversity or know where they exist, including for people who do not identify as queer.

**Podcasts, books and newsletters.** Poly podcasts and authors often mention events. You will find an overview in [polyamory podcasts and books](/en/blog/polyamory-podcasts-books/).

**Word of mouth.** Once you have found one group, someone there almost always knows the next one.

If there is nothing near you, look at the nearest bigger city or at online meetups. Some people also start a group themselves; more on that below.

## What to expect at your first meetup

Most meetups follow a similar pattern: one person organises, there is a short round of introductions with first names and sometimes a sentence about why you are there, and then conversations spread out. Newcomers are often approached on purpose.

A few tips for the first time:

- **Message the organiser beforehand.** A short note that you are new means someone will greet you.
- **You do not have to share anything.** Listening is completely fine. Nobody expects your life story.
- **Come alone or with a partner.** Both are common. Some groups have separate dates just for newcomers.
- **Do not expect a perfect group.** Every group has its own atmosphere. If it does not click the first time, try a second time or somewhere else.

## Common rules

Reputable groups usually have similar principles, often written down:

1. **Confidentiality:** What is shared at the meetup stays there. Nobody is outed outside it.
2. **No photos without consent**, and no names on social media.
3. **No hitting on people:** Meetups are not a pick-up venue. If you are interested in someone, ask respectfully and accept a no.
4. **Respect for all relationship styles:** There is no one right polyamory, whether hierarchical, solo poly or relationship anarchy.
5. **I-statements:** People talk about their own experiences, not about others.

If a group has no such rules and you feel uncomfortable, that is a legitimate reason not to return.

## Protecting your privacy

Not everyone can or wants to be openly polyamorous. A few measures help:

- Use only your first name or a nickname at first.
- Check what information is publicly visible in the group on social networks.
- Talk about your partners only in ways they would be comfortable with.
- Think beforehand about how you will react if you meet someone from work or your neighbourhood. Usually the answer is simple: the other person is there for the same reason.

More on this in [polyamory and privacy](/en/blog/polyamory-privacy/) and, if you are thinking about coming out, in [coming out as polyamorous](/en/blog/coming-out-polyamorous/).

## Starting a group yourself

If there is nothing in your area, you can start something yourself. It takes less than many people think:

- **A fixed place and rhythm**, for example every first Thursday of the month at the same café.
- **A simple announcement** on an event platform or in a regional group.
- **A few ground rules** that you briefly present at the meetup.
- **Patience.** The first dates are often small. Many groups only grow after a few months.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a polyamory meetup?**
A polyamory meetup is a regular, open gathering of people who are polyamorous or curious about it, usually in a café, bar or community space. It is about conversation and connection, not dating. New faces are usually explicitly welcome.

**How do I find poly meetups near me?**
Good places to look are event platforms such as Meetup, regional groups on social networks, subreddits for your city or region, LGBTQ+ centres and counselling services, and announcements in poly podcasts and forums. In smaller towns, look at the nearest bigger city or at online meetups.

**Do I have to be polyamorous to go to a meetup?**
No. Most meetups are also open to the curious, people in the process of opening up, monogamous people with polyamorous partners and anyone who wants to learn. Being respectful and listening is enough.

**Is a poly meetup a dating event?**
Usually not. Many groups explicitly ask people not to use meetups as a pick-up venue. Connections do sometimes form, of course, but anyone actively hunting for partners quickly comes across as pushy and drives others away.

**How do I protect my privacy at these meetups?**
Use only your first name or a nickname at first, ask before taking photos and do not share anything about others that they would not share themselves. Reputable groups have rules for this, such as nobody being outed outside the meetup.
