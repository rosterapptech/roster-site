**In short:** "Open relationship" and "polyamory" are often used interchangeably in everyday conversation, but they mean different things – and both are just two of several models within ethical non-monogamy (ENM). An open relationship is usually romantically exclusive but sexually open: a couple remains the emotional core, and everything else is an addition. Polyamory allows multiple simultaneous *romantic* relationships, often on equal footing. Alongside these sit polygamy, which is marriage-based and treated very differently under the law, and relationship anarchy, which rejects fixed hierarchies and categories altogether. This article maps out all four models, lays out the differences in a comparison table, and explains why the terms get thrown around loosely even though they describe quite different needs.

## The Open Relationship

In a classic open relationship there is a core couple that permits sexual contact outside the partnership – emotional bonds with others are usually not the goal. Agreements typically revolve around safer-sex rules, transparency, and what should be shared versus kept private. Some couples allow only one-off encounters with no repeats, others maintain recurring but deliberately unromantic connections with the same people. Some set geographic limits ("only while travelling"), others set time windows or rule out certain activities altogether, such as overnight stays or conventional dates.

The emotional priority almost always sits clearly with the core couple – that's what distinguishes the open relationship most sharply from the other three models. Where polyamory explicitly makes room for further love, an open relationship is mostly about allowing sexual freedom without shifting the emotional structure of the partnership at all.

## Polyamory

Polyamory literally means "many loves". Here, multiple simultaneous, emotionally significant relationships are explicitly welcomed – not just sexually, but romantically. Structures range from hierarchical (with one primary partnership, often a shared household, and further secondary connections) to egalitarian networks where all connections carry equal weight and none automatically takes precedence. Between these two poles sit countless in-between forms, shaped by living situation, housing, and personal needs – more on that below.

Unlike an open relationship, polyamory isn't about carving out an exception to exclusivity – it's about treating love itself as something that can grow rather than get divided up: affection for one person doesn't automatically reduce affection for another.

## Polygamy: The Historical Outlier

Polygamy is often confused with polyamory, not least because both words start with "poly" – but it means something fundamentally different. Polygamy is marriage-based: one person is simultaneously married to multiple spouses who are usually neither married to nor romantically involved with each other (most commonly polygyny, where one man has multiple wives; less commonly polyandry, where one woman has multiple husbands). The model is historically and religiously rooted and typically organised hierarchically and patriarchally – it's generally not built on mutual, equal consent among all the people involved with one another, the way polyamory aspires to be.

Legally, the distinction is stark: polygamous marriages aren't recognised in most Western legal systems, and in some places they're even a criminal offense, regardless of how consensually they're practised. Polyamory, by contrast, is usually legally unproblematic as a relationship style, because polyamorous people generally aren't seeking multiple legal marriages at all – they're living several partnerships in parallel without needing every connection to carry marital status. So the comparison doesn't really hold up: polygamy is about a – usually denied – state recognition of several simultaneous marriages, while for polyamory that question typically doesn't even come up, because the goal is a different one entirely.

## Relationship Anarchy: No Imposed Categories

Relationship anarchy goes a step further than egalitarian polyamory: it rejects any externally imposed hierarchy and fixed category – "partner" versus "just a friend," "more important" versus "less important" – outright. Every relationship is negotiated individually, regardless of whether sex or romance is involved, and regardless of what social convention might suggest instead. ## The Four Models Compared

| Criterion | Open Relationship | Polyamory | Polygamy | Relationship Anarchy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Emotional exclusivity | Yes (core couple) | No | Partial (per marriage) | No |
| Sexual exclusivity | No | No | No | No |
| Typical structure | One couple + outside contacts | Hierarchical to egalitarian | One person, multiple marriages | No fixed structure |
| Legal status | Core couple can marry | No multiple marriage sought | Multiple marriage usually unrecognised | Marriage optional, no hierarchy |
| Consent model | Couple sets the rules | All parties negotiate | Often patriarchal/hierarchical | Fully individually negotiated |

## And Within Polyamory Itself?

The table already hints at it: "polyamory" is itself an umbrella term for many different shapes, not one uniform model with fixed rules. Some constellations are [hierarchical, others non-hierarchical](/en/blog/hierarchical-vs-non-hierarchical-polyamory) – with or without a primary partnership, with or without a shared household at the centre. Some people practise [solo polyamory](/en/blog/solo-polyamory) and deliberately keep no single partnership at the centre of their life, staying independent while maintaining several relationships. Others organise as a kitchen-table polycule, where everyone involved knows each other and enjoys spending time together, while others prefer parallel relationships where the individual partnerships barely intersect. And some live as a triad with three equal partners. Anyone who wants to go deeper will find the details in the linked articles.

## Common Questions

**Is an open relationship the same as polyamory?**
No. Open relationships are usually sexually open but romantically exclusive – the core couple stays the emotional center. Polyamory allows multiple romantic relationships at once, often on equal footing.

**What legally separates polygamy from polyamory?**
Polygamy is a multiple-marriage arrangement and isn't recognized in most Western legal systems, and in some places it's even a criminal offense. Polyamory is a relationship style with no claim to multiple legal marriages, so the question of marriage recognition usually doesn't arise in practice.
