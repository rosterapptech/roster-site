**In short:** A throuple (also called a triad) is a relationship between three people where all three are romantically and/or sexually connected to each other. That's what distinguishes a throuple from a V structure, where one person (the "hinge") has two partners who aren't themselves connected. Throuples tend to work best when all three relationships are treated as equal, rather than an existing couple simply expanding to include a third person.

**Throuple** is one of those terms that social media popularized – it refers to a romantic relationship between exactly three people. The term sounds simple, but the dynamics behind it often aren't.

## Throuple vs. V structure: the key difference

In a true **throuple** (or closed triad), all three people are romantically and/or sexually connected to each other – A loves B, B loves C, and A loves C too. All three connections exist side by side as equals.

In a **V structure**, one person (the hinge) has two partners who don't themselves have a romantic or sexual relationship with each other. B is with A and with C, but A and C aren't connected to one another. Both structures are valid forms of polyamory – the difference simply lies in how the connections relate to each other.

## Throuple, triad, thruple: which term is right?

All three mean the same thing; they just come from different places. **Triad** is the older term from the polyamorous community and describes the structure neutrally. **Throuple** is a portmanteau of "three" and "couple" that became popular through social media and reality TV, which is why you meet it more often outside poly circles. **Thruple** is simply a common misspelling of throuple.

One distinction is still worth knowing: "triad" describes the structure neutrally, whether open or closed. "Throuple" colloquially tends to mean a closed three-person relationship. So if you use the word, it helps to say briefly which one you mean.

## How do throuples form?

There are essentially two paths:

**Together from the start.** Three people meet and develop feelings for each other in parallel, without a two-person relationship coming first.

**Out of an existing couple.** A couple opens up to a third person who becomes romantically connected to both of them. This path carries a structural risk: without deliberate effort, the third person can easily slide into a subordinate role while the original couple unconsciously keeps enjoying priority. In its pronounced form this pattern has its own name: [unicorn hunting](/en/blog/unicorn-hunting-polyamory/) – a couple goes looking for a third person who has to suit both of them and fit into an already finished plan.

## The most common throuple configurations

Not every three-person relationship works the same way. Three variants come up most often:

**The closed triad.** The three are exclusive with each other; there are no further romantic or sexual relationships outside. This is essentially [polyfidelity](/en/blog/polyfidelity/) for three.

**The open triad.** The three form the core, but some or all of them also have relationships outside it. That makes the throuple part of a larger [polycule](/en/blog/what-is-a-polycule/), and metamours enter the picture.

**The asymmetrical triad.** All three are connected, but not in the same form: two might live together while the third connection is romantic without being built around cohabitation. That is not a deficiency – as long as it is named rather than silently assumed.

## Common challenges in triads

**Uneven relationship intensity.** Rarely do all three connections develop at exactly the same pace or intensity – that's normal, but it should be discussed openly rather than implicitly demoting one person.

**Scheduling for three.** When do all three meet together, and when in pairs? Clear agreements prevent anyone from feeling excluded. More on systems for this in [Scheduling Multiple Relationships](/en/blog/scheduling-multiple-relationships/).

**Conflict dynamics between three people.** Conflicts between two people in a throuple automatically involve the third – that can help (more perspectives) or hurt (coalition-forming), depending on how openly it's communicated.

**The desire for equality.** Especially when a throuple grew out of an existing couple, it's worth asking deliberately: are we really treating this relationship as equal, or does the third person structurally remain "the addition"?

## Everyday life for three: living together, money and legal reality

What rarely shows up in posts about throuples, but makes the biggest difference day to day:

**Living arrangements.** Do all three move in together, or do two live together while the third lives alone? The second option is more common – and that is exactly where an imbalance creeps in, because two people automatically share more everyday time. Naming it openly makes it possible to counteract; see also [nesting partner](/en/blog/nesting-partner-explained/).

**Money.** Three incomes, three different financial starting points, often one shared home. Splitting rent three ways sounds simple but gets lopsided fast when only two names are on the lease. More on this in [finances in polyamory](/en/blog/finances-polyamory/).

**Law.** The unromantic part: in most countries only two people can marry or enter a registered partnership. For the third person that means no automatic visitation, information or inheritance rights when it matters. A lot of this can be arranged privately – power of attorney, a will, all names on the lease – but it does not happen by itself. If you are planning long term as three, discuss it early, ideally with qualified advice.

**Children.** Same picture here: parenting as three is lived reality, but legally it usually cannot be mapped onto three people. How families handle that is covered in [polyamory and parenting](/en/blog/polyamory-parenting/).

## What a good three-way balance needs

- **Open three-way communication instead of two-person talks about the third person.** Topics that affect all three belong in a shared conversation.
- **Deliberate use of time** between group time and pair time, without one pairing structurally disadvantaging another.
- **Regular, honest check-ins** on how each of the three connections is actually feeling – not just the triad as a whole.

## Not every throuple stays a threesome forever

Like any relationship structure, a triad can change: some stay stable for years, others evolve into a more open structure with more relationships, others split into separate relationships. None of these outcomes automatically means "failure" – see also [Breakups in Polyamory](/en/blog/breakups-polyamory/).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a throuple?**
A throuple is a relationship between three people, all of whom are romantically and/or sexually connected to each other.

**What's the difference between a throuple and a V structure?**
In a throuple, all three people are connected to each other. In a V structure, one person has two partners who aren't themselves in a relationship with each other.

**Are all three relationships in a throuple always equally strong?**
Not necessarily, and that's not required – what matters is talking about it openly, rather than implicitly treating one connection as less important.

**Can a couple open up to a triad without disadvantaging the third person?**
Yes, if there's deliberate attention paid to making sure the new person doesn't slide into a subordinate role and all three connections are treated as equal.

**Does a throuple have to stay a threesome forever?**
No. Like any relationship structure, a triad can change – evolving into more relationships or dissolving again, without that automatically meaning failure.

**What's the difference between a throuple and a triad?**
None in substance. "Triad" is the older, neutral term from the polyamorous community; "throuple" is the portmanteau of "three" and "couple" that social media made popular. "Thruple" is a common misspelling.

**Can three people get married?**
In most countries, no – marriage and registered partnership are limited to two people. Much can still be secured privately, for instance through power of attorney, a will, or a lease with all names on it.

**How do we start a throuple as a couple without unicorn hunting?**
By having the third person shape decisions from the start rather than moving into a finished plan: no rules set over their head in advance, no veto held by the original couple, and a willingness to let the existing relationship change too.
