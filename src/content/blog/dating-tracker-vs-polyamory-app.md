**In short:** Rostr, Spread, The Roster AI Dating Diary, and Roster: Date Smarter are four popular apps for logging your dating life. Based on their own App Store descriptions, all four are built for the getting-to-know-you phase — individual dates, vibes, and the question "is this a match?" None of them mention polyamory, polycules, or multiple concurrent, committed relationships. **Roster** picks up exactly where these four leave off: relationships that are long past the first date.

Anyone searching for a "polyamory app" or "best dating app 2026" now runs into a whole cluster of small, independently developed apps with similar names and similar promises: Rostr, Spread, The Roster AI Dating Diary, Roster: Date Smarter. That's confusing, especially since two of them literally have "Roster" in the name. This article honestly breaks down what these four apps do well, what they don't cover according to their own descriptions, and why people actually managing several relationships at once need a different tool.

## What the four apps actually are, according to the App Store

**Rostr – Dating Tracker** describes itself this way: "Turn your dating life into a story worth tracking." The app logs conversations, dates, and connections, including type, vibe, and outcome, plus expense tracking and an "AI wingman" for dating advice. Target audience: people getting to know several others and wanting to keep track of the *getting-to-know-you* phase.

**Spread: Dating Spreadsheet** calls itself "The Intentional Dating Companion." At its core, it's compatibility tracking with custom must-haves and dealbreakers, plus a "Connection Score" based on green, beige, and red flags. Again: a tool for evaluating and comparing matches, not for maintaining existing relationships.

**The Roster AI - Dating Diary** positions itself as a post-date reflection tool — chemistry, emotional safety, energy — with AI-assisted pattern recognition across multiple dates. Explicitly "no swiping, messaging, or other features," so it's deliberately limited to reflecting on individual dates.

**Roster: Date Smarter** describes itself as "the private dating CRM you didn't know you needed," with a tier system (A-tier, B-tier, private top-tier), interaction tracking, and a comparison view between several people you're currently getting to know.

All four are solid tools for what they were built to do. But not one of the four official descriptions mentions the word "polyamory," "polycule," or "ethical non-monogamy" even once.

## The naming confusion: two apps are called "Roster"

One detail that causes confusion: both "Roster: Date Smarter" and "The Roster AI - Dating Diary" carry the term "Roster" in their name — and neither is the same app as the one described here. The term "Roster" originally comes from sports (a team's player list) and evolved into dating slang via TikTok, see [What Is a Dating Roster?](/en/blog/what-is-a-dating-roster). It's therefore no surprise that several independent developers landed on the same name. Anyone searching for "Roster app" should look closely at which of the several similarly named apps is actually meant, and what it was built for.

## The real gap: tracking vs. actually maintaining relationships

The pattern across all four apps is the same: they help structure the *search* — who you met, how the date went, whether the person fits your criteria. That's a real problem, and these apps solve it well.

But once getting to know someone turns into an actual, ongoing relationship — several of them at once, each with its own history, agreements, and dynamic — a tier system or a connection score quickly stops being useful. None of the four tools offer:

- A **network view** showing how several concurrent relationships relate to each other.
- A system for **agreements** between everyone involved — safer sex, communication rhythms, boundaries.
- **Safer-sex and testing tracking** built for several parallel, ongoing partners rather than individual dates.
- An **honest status check per relationship** showing which connection currently needs attention — not which date went best.

This isn't a knock on these apps. They were built for the getting-to-know-you phase, and they're well designed for it. It's just that "getting to know and evaluating several people" is a different problem than "keeping several relationships healthy at once."

## Privacy: a point where the four apps genuinely differ

Especially with relationship data, it's worth checking the privacy details listed in the App Store yourself before choosing (as of July 2026; App Store privacy details can change with future app updates): not all of the apps in this comparison make the same claims, and the differences can matter.

If you're journaling about dates, chemistry, and emotional details, it's worth knowing where that data goes — and the most reliable way to check is the current App Store privacy listing of the app you're considering, not just a blog post. This is especially relevant when your own relationships already require discretion, whether toward family, an employer, or in custody matters.

## Where Roster picks up

**Roster** isn't meant to replace Rostr, Spread, or similar trackers — if you're currently getting to know several people and want to organize exactly that phase, a dedicated dating tracker serves you well. Roster picks up a step later: once getting to know someone has turned into actual, parallel relationships that last weeks or years.

**Connections.** For every person you're in a relationship with, a dedicated space for details, where conversations stand, and the history of your connection — not for individual dates, but for the relationship as a whole.

**Polycule Map.** A network view of your entire polycule with you at the center: close connections nearer, looser ones further out. Exactly the feature missing from Rostr, Spread, and similar apps once more than one relationship is running at the same time.

**Vibe Radar.** An honest weather report per relationship — from sunny to radio silence — so you can see which connection currently needs attention, instead of only noticing once someone already feels neglected.

**Wellness.** A private space for safer-sex appointments and test results, built for several parallel, ongoing partners rather than individual dates.

**Reflection.** A journal for your own perspective on each individual relationship.

Everything stays local on your device, with no account and no cloud. [Roster is available on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/roster).
