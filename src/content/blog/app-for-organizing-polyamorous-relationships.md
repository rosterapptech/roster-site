**In short:** Dating apps like Feeld or helloPOLY solve a different problem than an app for organizing relationships. Dating apps help you find new people — matching, browsing, first conversations. Once you're actually in multiple relationships, though, you don't need another swiping interface — you need somewhere that keeps track of time, details, agreements, and relationship health. That's exactly the gap **Roster** fills: an app for polyamory that runs entirely on your device, with no account and no tracking, for one of the most sensitive categories of data there is.

Search for "an app for polyamory" and you'll almost always land on dating apps first. That's not a coincidence, but it's only half the answer. "Polyamory" describes two fairly different moments in life: the moment you want to meet new people, and the moment you've already done that and now need to actually keep several relationships running. There are good apps for the first moment by now. For the second, there's almost nothing. This article sorts the two apart.

## Two questions that keep getting conflated

"How do I meet people who are open to polyamory?" and "How do I keep track of the relationships I already have?" sound similar but are completely different problems. The first is a search-and-matching task. The second is an organizing task. Expecting one app to do both is a bit like expecting a matchmaking service to also run your shared calendar with your partner. Both matter — they're just different tools for different phases of life.

## What dating apps genuinely do well for poly people

This isn't meant to talk down apps like **Feeld** or **helloPOLY** — they solved a real need. A few years ago, there was barely a place where you could show up honestly as polyamorous or open to ethical non-monogamy without having to explain or hide it. These apps are good at what they were built for:

- **Matching by relationship structure.** You can state that you're polyamorous, open, or solo poly, and stop being squeezed into a monogamy-shaped mold.
- **Browsing and meeting people.** Profiles, photos, first messages — the classic dating app mechanics, just with room for more than one relationship at a time.
- **A sense of community.** For many people, simply not being the only one with this relationship structure is valuable in itself.

That's a real and important function. It's just a different function from the one this article is about.

## What dating apps don't solve

Once getting to know someone turns into a real, ongoing relationship — with its own history, its own agreements, its own dynamics — a dating app quickly becomes irrelevant. It was never built to tell you whether a three-month-old relationship needs attention right now. It knows nothing about safer sex agreements or communication rhythms. It has no place for the question of when you last really spent time with someone, or how a connection is currently feeling.

That's not a criticism of these apps — they were never meant for this. But if you're managing two, three, or four relationships at once, you notice quickly: the actual problem is no longer finding someone. The actual problem is keeping track, without a connection quietly getting neglected while you're focused on someone new.

## A different category of tool

This is exactly where **Roster** comes in — not as a replacement for Feeld, helloPOLY, or Tinder, but as a companion to them. The idea is simple: a dating app brings you together with people. Roster then helps you actually organize those relationships once they become real. Plenty of users run both side by side — a dating app for meeting people, Roster for everything that comes after. It's not about which app is "better." They're two different tools for two different phases.

## Privacy: the blind spot in most dating apps

There's a point that often gets overlooked, even though it matters especially in polyamory: relationship data is among the most sensitive data there is. Who's with whom, what agreements are in place, what your safer sex status looks like — this is information that can, in the worst case, touch your job, your family, or custody arrangements.

Many dating apps are monetized through advertising and data use. In practice, that means an account stored server-side, tracking across ad networks, and a lifespan for your own data that's hard to trace once it's out of your hands. This isn't an accusation aimed at any specific provider — it's simply how the industry tends to work. Most free apps have to fund themselves somehow, and data is a convenient currency for that.

For an app that goes on to organize your actual relationships, a different standard should apply. That's why **Roster** stores absolutely everything locally on your device. There's no server holding your data, no account to create, and no tracking running in the background. If you already hesitate to put "polyamorous" publicly on a dating profile, you should hesitate even more before storing the details of your actual relationships on someone else's servers.

## What Roster actually offers

Once your relationships move past the initial getting-to-know-you stage, Roster takes over:

**Connections.** Every person you're in a relationship with gets their own space for details, conversation history, and the arc of your connection. A quick swipe is all it takes to log that you saw someone today — no elaborate protocol, just an honest snapshot of how often you're actually seeing each other.

**Polycule Map.** Your entire network at a glance, shown as an ego network centered on you: close connections sit nearer, looser ones further out. Genuinely useful once your polycule grows past two or three people and stops fitting neatly in your head.

**Vibe-Radar.** An honest weather reading for each relationship — from sunny to cloudy to rainy to radio silence. At a glance, you can see which connection could use some attention, instead of only noticing once someone already feels neglected.

**Reflection journal.** A space for your own perspective on each relationship — thoughts, doubts, joy, things you still need to work through. Somewhere to sort your own head before you walk into a conversation.

**Wellness.** A private section for safer sex appointments and test results, visible only to you, so you never lose track of when your next check-in is due.

All of it local, with no account, no cloud, nothing ever reaching a server. [Roster is available on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/apple-store/id6786782804?pt=129111138&ct=Website&mt=8).
