**In short:** Kitchen table polyamory and parallel polyamory describe how closely the people in a [polycule](/blog/what-is-a-polycule) are connected to each other. In kitchen table polyamory, everyone figuratively sits at the same kitchen table – you know each other, spend time together, and stay in touch even with [metamours](/blog/metamour-relationships). In parallel polyamory, the relationships run independently side by side, with little to no direct contact between your partners' partners. Neither is "better" – what matters is what fits your personality and your life.

Once a polycule includes more than two people, the question eventually comes up: how much contact should actually exist between everyone involved? That question has gotten a name – kitchen table polyamory and parallel polyamory are the two best-known answers.

## What is kitchen table polyamory?

The name comes from the image of the kitchen table: everyone in the polycule could, in theory, sit together at one table, drink coffee, and chat – that's how familiar the dynamic is. That doesn't necessarily mean everyone has breakfast together daily, but:

- People know each other personally and maintain a friendly to warm relationship.
- Shared activities (celebrations, trips, or just an evening with three or more people) are normal and welcome.
- Information flows openly – nobody has to constantly translate or relay messages, because everyone is in direct contact.
- Metamours ideally become friends, or at least trusted acquaintances.

## What is parallel polyamory?

In parallel polyamory, the individual relationships deliberately run alongside each other without overlapping:

- People know about each other and respect the other connections, but have little to no personal contact with metamours.
- Agreements and information usually go through the shared partner rather than directly between everyone involved.
- Each relationship gets its own space, without the contexts blending together.
- This reduces complexity, but can also make it feel like the partner is living "separate lives."

## Which model fits whom?

**Kitchen table tends to fit well if:**
- you draw energy from shared time with many people rather than losing it.
- transparency and a "we're all a team" feeling matter to you.
- your metamours have similar social needs to your own.

**Parallel tends to fit well if:**
- you're introverted or get overwhelmed quickly by lots of social contact.
- your relationships have very different contexts (different cities, life stages, communities).
- you and your partners deliberately want more privacy within each connection.

Important: the decision is never up to just one person. One partner might want kitchen table while another clearly prefers parallel – that calls for a negotiated compromise, not a model forced on everyone.

## The middle ground: garden party polyamory

Between the two extremes sits what's called garden party polyamory: people occasionally meet at bigger events (birthdays, celebrations) but don't maintain close everyday contact. For many polycules, this is a realistic middle ground – especially when everyone involved generally likes each other but has different needs around closeness.

## Can the model change over time?

Yes, and that's entirely normal. A polycule that starts out with parallel structures sometimes shifts toward kitchen table over months or years – for instance, when initial reserve turns into genuine liking between metamours. Just as easily, a kitchen-table setup can loosen up again as life circumstances change. No model is set in stone.

## Common pitfalls with both models

**With kitchen table:** the pressure to like everyone. Closeness should develop naturally, not be forced – "we're all friends" doesn't work as a top-down announcement.

**With parallel:** the shared partner becomes the sole source of information and gets overloaded. Even in parallel polyamory, a minimum of direct contact is worth having, for logistical coordination if nothing else.

## Frequently asked questions

**What's the difference between kitchen table and parallel polyamory?**
In kitchen table polyamory, everyone maintains a close, friendly relationship with each other, including metamours. In parallel polyamory, the relationships run independently side by side, with little to no direct contact between your partners' partners.

**Which model is better?**
Neither. Both are valid, functional approaches – which one fits better depends on personality, social capacity, and the wishes of everyone involved.

**Do you have to pick one model?**
No, many polycules land somewhere in between, or use so-called garden party polyamory as a middle ground with occasional contact at bigger events.

**What if I want kitchen table but my partner prefers parallel?**
That's a normal tension, not a sign of a broken relationship model. It calls for a negotiated compromise rather than one side's model being imposed on the other.

**Can parallel polyamory turn into kitchen table over time?**
Yes, that happens fairly often as initial distance turns into genuine liking between the people involved. Contact can just as easily loosen up again.
