**In short:** A Dating Roster is the group of people someone is casually dating in parallel right now – without it automatically implying exclusivity, commitment, or a serious relationship. The term comes from sports (a team's "roster") and became dating slang via TikTok, used to casually and honestly describe your current dating situation: "Who's on your roster right now?"

Few dating terms have spread as fast in recent years as **Roster Dating**. What started on TikTok as a tongue-in-cheek insider term is now firmly part of a whole generation's dating vocabulary. But what does Dating Roster actually mean – and how is it different from concepts like a situationship or polyamory?

## Dating Roster: Meaning and Origin

The term **Roster** originally comes from sports, where it refers to the list of players on a team – who's currently "on the roster." In a dating context, the metaphor works almost identically: your Dating Roster is the list of people you're currently meeting up with, texting, or dating, without each of them being a committed relationship.

The term went mainstream via TikTok, where creators humorously described their own dating situation as a "roster update" or joked about "who's currently on my roster." The tone is deliberately light – it takes some of the heaviness out of dating multiple people at once, heaviness that words like "unfaithful" or "indecisive" tend to carry.

## What Belongs on a Dating Roster – and What Doesn't

A Dating Roster typically consists of:

- **People in the early stages of getting to know each other**, where it's still open whether things become something more serious.
- **Casual dates with no expectation of exclusivity**, where both sides know it isn't (yet) exclusive.
- **Several parallel connections of varying intensity** – from "we text occasionally" to "we see each other regularly."

Established, exclusive relationships generally don't belong on the roster – once exclusivity has been discussed and agreed on, a person leaves the roster, conceptually, and becomes a partner.

## Dating Roster vs. Situationship vs. Polyamory

These three terms are often mixed up, but they mean different things:

**Dating Roster** describes the phase of getting to know several people at once, usually without defined labels or expectations. It's more of a state than a relationship model.

**Situationship** refers to a single connection that feels like a relationship but was never officially named as one – often with unclear expectations and uncertainty on at least one side.

**Polyamory** is a deliberately chosen relationship model built on open communication, involving multiple simultaneous, usually emotionally committed relationships – unlike a roster, which is often still casual and exploratory. Read more on the distinction in [Polyamory vs. Open Relationship](/en/blog/polyamory-vs-open-relationship/).

A Dating Roster can therefore evolve over time toward polyamory, if several loose connections deliberately grow into committed multiple relationships – or it can narrow down to a single relationship once one connection becomes exclusive.

## Why the Term Resonates So Much

The success of "Dating Roster" lies in the fact that it names a real dating experience – getting to know several people in parallel – without shame or stigma. Instead of justifying "indecisiveness," your dating situation is treated for what it is: a deliberate phase of exploring.
